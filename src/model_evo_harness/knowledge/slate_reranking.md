# Slate reranking and diversity

A pointwise ranker scores each item independently; a slate method evaluates order, competition, diversity, and constraints across a displayed set. PRM and PRS are examples of context/position-aware reranking. A score gain is irrelevant if the candidate slate cannot be assembled under the real inventory and policy rules.

## Data and conditions

Require logged candidate sets, displayed positions, slate-level feedback, eligibility constraints, and a way to evaluate changed rankings. Position affects exposure, so clicked items alone do not identify the value of a new order. A binary coupon/no-coupon action has no slate unless the host explicitly defines one.

## Controlled experiment

Freeze candidate generation and pointwise scores. Compare score sorting, a simple diversity or business-rule reranker, and one learned slate model under the same feasible candidates and top-K. Measure slate utility, item coverage, constraint violations, latency, and uncertainty. If logs were generated by one narrow policy, acknowledge that counterfactual support may be inadequate.

## Interpretation and feature needs

When the effect exists only through position, acquire position and impression logs before modeling; domain knowledge can justify this missing-data request immediately. If pointwise ranking remains the bottleneck, a slate architecture adds complexity without addressing the observed error.

## Method choices

| Method | What changes | First discriminating test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PRM | Transformer-style interactions across slate positions. | Freeze candidates and compare with independent score sorting. |
| PRS | Models position response and continuation, then searches permutations. | Compare with a simple constrained permutation under the same logged support. |
